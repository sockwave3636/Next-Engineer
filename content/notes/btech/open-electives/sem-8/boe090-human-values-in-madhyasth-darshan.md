# BOE090 Human Values in Madhyasth Darshan: One-Day Exam Revision Notes

Original revision notes written for the AKTU syllabus, based on the Madhyasth Darshan (Sah-astitvavad, "Co-existentialism") of A. Nagraj and the Universal Human Values course. Every unit has the same parts:
1. **2-mark definitions**: short answers you can write word for word in Section A.
2. **Core concepts**: the framework explained step by step, with tables and flow diagrams.
3. **Examples and comparisons**: everyday situations that show the ideas.
4. **Key points to remember**.
5. **Important questions**: the long questions to prepare, with what your answer must contain.

## How to use these notes the day before the paper

- **Morning (3 hours):** Unit 1 (why study it, the basic formulations, the literature, scope and outcomes) and Unit 2 (existence as units submerged in space, material and consciousness, the four orders, evolution).
- **Afternoon (3 hours):** Unit 3 (human being as part of nature, five classes of humans, self and body, realization and prosperity) and Unit 4 (natural, social and psychological principles, conducive social order, self-study).
- **Evening (2 hours):** Unit 5 (conduct of a realized human, continuity of happiness, present-day problems), then the "Last-hour checklist".
- **In the exam:** use the course's terms (with the Hindi word in brackets once), draw the tables of the four orders and the flow diagrams, and give a real-life example. Explain and reason; do not just preach.

**AKTU paper pattern (theory, 70 marks, 3 hours):** Section A has 7 short questions of 2 marks, all compulsory (14). Section B has 5 questions of 7 marks, attempt any 3 (21). Section C has 5 questions (one per unit), each with two parts, attempt one part of each, 7 marks each (35). Every unit gets its own Section C question, so do not skip any unit.

## Unit 1: Introduction to Madhyasth Darshan

### 2-mark definitions

- **Madhyasth Darshan:** a philosophy (darshan = way of seeing reality) propounded by A. Nagraj (1920–2016) of Amarkantak, also called **Sah-astitvavad (Co-existentialism)**, which holds that existence is co-existence and that every human can understand it and live in harmony.
- **Madhyasth:** "in the middle", balanced; the state that is neither excess nor deficiency; the self-organized, harmonious state at the centre of every unit.
- **Darshan:** seeing reality as it is, through understanding (not belief).
- **Co-existence (sah-astitva):** the state in which all units exist together in space, related and mutually fulfilling.
- **Jeevan Vidya:** the education programme based on Madhyasth Darshan that helps people explore the self, relationships and nature; the Universal Human Values (UHV) course draws on it.
- **Self-exploration:** the process of verifying proposals on the basis of one's own natural acceptance and then living by them (experiential validation).
- **Natural acceptance:** the innate acceptance in every human of what is right (harmony, relationship, trust), which is constant, universal and not dependent on belief.
- **Realization (anubhav):** the state of understanding co-existence completely, from which right thought and behaviour flow.
- **Resolution (samadhan):** being free of confusion and contradiction in the self; the basis of happiness.
- **Prosperity (samriddhi):** the feeling of having more than enough physical facilities for one's rightly identified needs.

### Core concepts

**Need for studying Madhyasth Darshan**
- Humans want continuous happiness and prosperity, but present education gives skills without the understanding of what to do with them.
- Problems at every level (stress, broken families, conflict, war, environmental damage) come from lack of right understanding of the self, relationships and nature.
- Earlier answers were based on belief (idealism, religion) or on matter alone (materialism) and could not be verified by everyone; Madhyasth Darshan offers proposals to be verified by each person through self-exploration.
- It aims at a universal (sarvabhaum) human order, not limited to any community, region or religion.

**Basic formulations (core proposals)**
1. **Existence is co-existence:** units (prakriti) are submerged in space (satta); space and units are inseparable.
2. **Everything in existence is in harmony and order**; only humans, by not understanding, create disorder.
3. **Existence is of two kinds of units: material (jad) and consciousness (chaitanya).**
4. **Nature is in four orders:** material, pranic (plant), animal and human, which are interconnected and mutually fulfilling.
5. **A human being is the co-existence of the self (jeevan, a conscious unit) and the body (sharir, material).**
6. **Every human has the same basic aspiration**: continuous happiness (resolution) and prosperity.
7. **The program:** right understanding (in the self) + right feelings in relationship + physical facilities from nature.
8. **Human goal:** resolution in every individual, prosperity in every family, fearlessness (trust) in society, co-existence in nature.

**Literature of Madhyasth Darshan (structure)**

| Group | Texts |
|---|---|
| Four darshans | Manav Vyavahar Darshan (human conduct), Manav Karma Darshan (human work), Manav Abhyas Darshan (practice), Manav Anubhav Darshan (realization) |
| Three vaads (views) | Samadhanatmak Bhautikvad (resolution-based materialism), Vyavaharatmak Janvad (behaviour-based humanism), Anubhavatmak Adhyatmavad (realization-based spiritualism) |
| Three shastras (sciences) | Avartanshil Arthashastra (cyclic economics), Vyavaharvadi Samajshastra (behavioural sociology), Manav Sanchetnavadi Manovigyan (human-consciousness psychology) |
| Others | Manaviya Samvidhan (human constitution), Jeevan Vidya: Ek Parichay |

**Madhyasth Darshan vs earlier views**

| View | Basis | Limitation (in the course's view) |
|---|---|---|
| Idealism / belief-based spiritualism | Faith, scripture; the world is illusion | Not verifiable by all; divides by belief |
| Materialism (science-based) | Matter is all; consciousness a product of matter | Gives control over things but no answer to "how to live"; consumerism |
| Madhyasth Darshan (co-existentialism) | Understanding of existence as co-existence, verified by self-exploration | Needs sincere self-exploration and practice |

**Scope:** the self (understanding, feelings, thoughts), family (relationships), society (undivided society), nature (four orders), existence (co-existence); applies to education, health, production, exchange and justice; to profession and technology.

**Outcomes of living by Madhyasth Darshan:** clarity and resolution in the self; trust-based relationships; prosperity with limited needs; ethical professional conduct; harmony with nature (sustainable living); a contribution to undivided society and universal human order.

### Examples and comparisons

**Example 1: Belief vs understanding.** A student follows a rule "do not lie" because a book says so (belief); under pressure it breaks. Another sees for themself that lying breaks trust, which they naturally want in every relationship (understanding through self-exploration). The second student's honesty is stable.

**Example 2: Natural acceptance test.** Ask anyone: "Do you want a relationship based on trust or on suspicion?" Everyone, everywhere, accepts trust. Such universal answers are the basis for proposals in the darshan.

### Key points to remember

- Madhyasth Darshan = Sah-astitvavad = Co-existentialism, by A. Nagraj; Jeevan Vidya is its education.
- Formulations: co-existence, four orders, self + body, aspiration, program, human goal.
- Literature: 4 darshans, 3 vaads, 3 shastras, Manaviya Samvidhan.
- Verify by natural acceptance and experiential validation, not by belief.

### Important questions

1. What is Madhyasth Darshan? Explain the need to study it.
2. Explain the basic formulations of Madhyasth Darshan.
3. Describe the literature (darshans, vaads, shastras) of Madhyasth Darshan.
4. Compare Madhyasth Darshan with idealism and materialism.
5. Explain the scope and outcomes of living according to Madhyasth Darshan.

## Unit 2: Nature as Existence Submerged in Space

### 2-mark definitions

- **Existence (astitva):** everything that is: all units (nature) together with all-pervading space; existence = co-existence.
- **Space (satta / shunya / vyapak):** the all-pervading, transparent, limitless reality in which all units are submerged; it is not empty nothingness but the medium that energizes (urja) and connects all units.
- **Unit (ikai):** anything that is limited in size and has activity: an atom, a plant, an animal, a human.
- **Submerged (sampṛkt):** every unit is soaked in space, energized by it, and not separate from it.
- **Material unit (jad ikai):** a unit that has activity but no knowing (atoms and their combinations: soil, water, metals, bodies).
- **Conscious unit (chaitanya ikai / jeevan):** a constitutionally complete atom (gathanpurna parmanu) that has the activity of knowing, assuming, recognizing and fulfilling; the self.
- **Four orders (char avasthayein):** material order (padarth avastha), pranic/plant order (pran avastha), animal order (jeev avastha), human order (gyan avastha).
- **Innateness (dharma):** the quality of a unit that is always with it and cannot be separated from it (e.g. existence for soil).
- **Natural characteristic (svabhav):** the participation of a unit in the larger order (its value).
- **Cyclicity and mutual fulfilment (avartanshilta, paraspar purakta):** each order fulfils the others in a cycle (soil nourishes plants, plants become soil again), with no waste.

### Core concepts

**Existence = units submerged in space**
- Units are many (prakriti); space is one (satta).
- Units are limited; space is unlimited; units are active; space is "no-activity" but energizes.
- Every unit is self-organized and recognizes and fulfils its relationship with other units; hence existence is in harmony.
- Space is the source of self-organization (energized-ness) of units.

**Material and consciousness**

| Aspect | Material (jad) | Consciousness (chaitanya / jeevan) |
|---|---|---|
| Example | Atom, soil, water, body | The self ("I") |
| Activity | Recognizing and fulfilling (physical, chemical) | Knowing, assuming, recognizing, fulfilling |
| Growth in quantity | Yes (composition, decomposition) | No (an atomic unit, does not grow or decay) |
| Needs | Physical (of the body) | Happiness (of the self) |
| Continuity | Temporary forms | Continuous |
| Measurable | Quantitatively | Qualitatively (feelings, understanding) |

**The four orders (draw the table)**

| Order | Things | Activity | Innateness (dharma) | Natural characteristic (svabhav) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Material (padarth) | Soil, water, metals, air | Composition and decomposition | Existence | Composition/decomposition (gives to other orders) |
| Pranic (pran) | Plants, trees | Composition/decomposition + respiration (pranic activity) | Existence + growth | Nurture / worsen (nourishes and gives) |
| Animal (jeev) | Animals, birds | Above + selection by the self, based on sensation | Existence + growth + will to live | Non-cruelty (also cruelty) |
| Human (gyan) | Human beings | Above + knowing, assuming, recognizing, fulfilling | Existence + growth + will to live + will to live with happiness | Perseverance (dhiraj), bravery (veerta), generosity (udarta); values: kindness (daya), beneficence (kripa), compassion (karuna) |

**Interconnectedness and mutual fulfilment**

| Order | Fulfils material | Fulfils pranic | Fulfils animal | Fulfils human |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Material | Mutual (soil–water–air) | Soil and water nourish plants | Soil, water, air for animals | Minerals, metals, soil, water |
| Pranic | Leaves and decay enrich soil | Plants support each other (shade, pollination) | Food for animals | Food, medicine, fibre, timber |
| Animal | Excreta enrich soil | Pollination, seed dispersal | Food chains | Milk, transport, help in farming |
| Human | Should enrich soil (not pollute) | Should nurture plants (not deforest) | Should care (not exploit) | Should fulfil relationships (justice) |

Only humans are not yet fulfilling to the other three orders, because of lack of right understanding.

**Evolution (vikas) in existence**
- **Physical evolution:** atoms → molecules → molecular structures → material order → pranic cell → plants → animal bodies → human body (with richer, more complex structures).
- **Evolution in consciousness:** when an atom becomes **constitutionally complete** (gathanpurna), it becomes a conscious unit (jeevan); it then works through bodies: first in animal consciousness (jeev chetna), and, with understanding, progresses to human consciousness (manav chetna), godly consciousness (dev chetna) and divine consciousness (divya chetna).
- Material evolution is in quantity and structure; conscious evolution is in understanding (quality).

### Examples and comparisons

**Example 1: Cyclic farming.** In a traditional farm, cow dung and crop waste go back to the soil; the soil grows fodder and grain; animals and humans eat; waste returns. The cycle is mutually fulfilling. Chemical farming breaks the cycle: soil fertility drops and water gets polluted.

**Example 2: Recognizing and fulfilling.** Water (material) recognizes and fulfils its relationship with a plant by being absorbed; a cow (animal) recognizes grass as food by sensation; a human can recognize a relationship with another human through understanding and fulfil it with trust and respect.

### Key points to remember

- Existence = units (prakriti) submerged in space (satta) = co-existence.
- Two kinds of units: material (jad) and consciousness (chaitanya/jeevan).
- Four orders with things, activity, innateness, natural characteristic (table).
- Mutual fulfilment and cyclicity; humans currently not fulfilling.
- Evolution: material (structure) and consciousness (jeev → manav → dev → divya chetna).

### Important questions

1. Explain existence as co-existence of units submerged in space.
2. Distinguish between material units and conscious units.
3. Explain the four orders of nature with their activity, innateness and natural characteristic.
4. Explain interconnectedness and mutual fulfilment among the four orders.
5. Explain evolution in existence according to Madhyasth Darshan.

## Unit 3: The Human Being in Nature

### 2-mark definitions

- **Human being:** the co-existence of the self (jeevan, conscious) and the body (sharir, material); the self is the seer, doer and enjoyer, and the body is an instrument of the self.
- **Self (jeevan / "I"):** the conscious unit that has desires, thoughts and expectations, and whose need is happiness.
- **Body (sharir):** the material unit whose needs are physical facilities (food, clothing, shelter) and which works as an instrument of the self.
- **Sanyam (self-regulation):** the feeling of responsibility in the self for nurturing, protecting and rightly using the body.
- **Swasthya (health):** the body acting according to the self, with all parts in harmony.
- **Activities of the self:** ten activities in five pairs: tasting and selecting (mann), analysing and comparing (vritti), contemplating and imaging (chitta), understanding and resolving (buddhi), realizing and expressing it authentically (atma).
- **Animal consciousness (jeev chetna):** living for physical facilities and sensory pleasure, as animals do.
- **Human consciousness (manav chetna):** living with right understanding, justice, resolution and prosperity.
- **Realization (anubhav):** knowing existence as co-existence; the deepest activity of the self from which continuous happiness flows.
- **Samadhan-samriddhi (resolution with prosperity):** the human goal at the individual and family level.

### Core concepts

**Human being as an indivisible part of nature:** humans depend on the other three orders for their body and on relationships for the self; they are related to all units. Only humans have the possibility of understanding the whole of existence; hence their role is to complete the harmony of nature by fulfilling relationships with all.

**Self and body (draw it):**

| Self (jeevan) | Body (sharir) |
|---|---|
| Needs happiness (sukh) | Needs physical facilities (suvidha) |
| Needs are qualitative | Needs are quantitative |
| Fulfilled by right understanding and right feelings | Fulfilled by physical facilities |
| Continuous need | Temporary, limited need |
| Activities: desire, thought, expectation; knowing, assuming, recognizing, fulfilling | Activities: recognizing and fulfilling (digestion, breathing) |
| Continuous | Temporary |

Exchange: self → body (information, decisions); body → self (sensations). Problems arise when we try to get happiness from the body's sensations alone.

**Ten activities of the self (jeevan)**

| Power (bal) | Activity at the level of | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| Mann (mind) | Tasting (ashwadan), selecting (chayan) | Likes and selection |
| Vritti | Analysing (vishleshan), comparing (tulan) | Thought |
| Chitta | Contemplating (chintan), imaging (chitran) | Desire/imagination |
| Buddhi | Understanding (bodh), resolving (sankalp) | Understanding |
| Atma | Realization (anubhav), authentic expression (pramanikta) | Realization of co-existence |

When the inner activities (realization, understanding) guide the outer ones (desire, thought, selection), the self is in harmony (resolution). When the outer activities are driven by sensations from outside, there is conflict.

**Five classes of humans (by level of consciousness)**

| Class | Consciousness | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Animal-like human (pashu manav) | Animal consciousness (jeev chetna) | Lives for sensation and physical facilities; fear-driven; insecurity |
| Demonic human (rakshas manav) | Animal consciousness, with exploitation | Gains at others' cost; cruelty, exploitation, deceit |
| Human (manav) | Human consciousness (manav chetna) | Justice in relationships, resolution, prosperity, right utilization of nature |
| Godly human (dev manav) | Godly consciousness (dev chetna) | Lives for others' welfare beyond the family; helps others understand |
| Divine human (divya manav) | Divine consciousness (divya chetna) | Complete realization; lives entirely for universal good |

The movement from animal-like to human and beyond is through understanding (education-sanskar), not through force or belief.

**Purpose of human life: realization and prosperity**
- **Realization (anubhav)** in the self → resolution → continuous happiness.
- **Prosperity (samriddhi)** in the family → right identification of needs (limited) + production of more than needed (with nature, cyclic) → feeling of having more than enough.
- Together: **samadhan-samriddhi** in individual and family; **abhay** (fearlessness) in society; **sah-astitva** (co-existence) in nature.

### Examples and comparisons

**Example 1: Sanyam and health.** A student who eats junk food late at night for taste (sensation) harms the body; with sanyam they choose food for nourishment and rest, the body stays healthy, and the self is not in conflict.

**Example 2: Moving between classes.** A businessperson who cheats customers (demonic level) starts seeing the customer as related and offers fair prices and quality (human level). Profit continues, trust grows; the change came from understanding, not from fear of punishment.

**Example 3: Needs.** The need for food is limited (a few kilograms a day); the need for respect is continuous. Trying to get respect through more food, clothes or cars (physical facilities) never satisfies, because the need is of the self.

### Key points to remember

- Human = self (jeevan) + body (sharir); self needs happiness, body needs facilities.
- Ten activities of the self in five pairs (mann, vritti, chitta, buddhi, atma).
- Five classes: pashu, rakshas, manav, dev, divya manav (animal → divine consciousness).
- Purpose: realization (resolution) + prosperity; sanyam leads to health.

### Important questions

1. Explain the human being as an indivisible part of nature.
2. Explain the human being as the co-existence of self and body, with their needs and activities.
3. Explain the five classes of humans according to Madhyasth Darshan.
4. Explain the activities of the self (jeevan) and the meaning of harmony in the self.
5. What is the purpose of human life? Explain realization and prosperity.

## Unit 4: Principles for Achieving the Human Goal

### 2-mark definitions

- **Human goal:** resolution in every individual, prosperity in every family, fearlessness (trust) in society and co-existence in nature.
- **Natural principle:** existence is co-existence; the four orders are mutually fulfilling and cyclic, so human production must be cyclic and enriching.
- **Social principle:** relationships exist and are to be recognized and fulfilled; justice (nyaya) leads to mutual happiness and an undivided society.
- **Psychological principle:** the self is happy when it is in harmony; realization → understanding → thought → behaviour and work in harmony.
- **Justice (nyaya):** recognition of a relationship, its fulfilment, and right evaluation, leading to mutual happiness.
- **Undivided society (akhand samaj):** a society of people related through trust, without divisions of caste, religion, gender, nation or wealth.
- **Universal human order (sarvabhaum vyavastha):** a human order from family to world family organized on five dimensions to fulfil the human goal.
- **Five dimensions of human order:** education-sanskar, health-sanyam, production-work, justice-preservation, exchange-storage.
- **Self-study (svadhyay):** exploring oneself through the proposals, verifying them on natural acceptance, and observing one's own living.
- **Family order (parivar vyavastha):** the smallest unit of human order, about 10 people, where relationships and work are organized.

### Core concepts

**Natural principles (with nature):**
- Recognize relationship with the material, pranic and animal orders; ensure their enrichment, protection and right utilization.
- Production should be **cyclic** (avartanshil): what is taken from nature is returned (organic farming, renewable energy, recycling).
- Needs are limited and identified; production more than needs → prosperity without exploitation.

**Social principles (with humans):**
- Relationships are naturally present; the nine feelings: trust (foundation), respect, affection, care, guidance, reverence, glory, gratitude, love (complete value).
- Justice from family to world family; trust leads to fearlessness.
- Family order → village/community order → … → world family order (expanding circles of trust).

**Psychological principles (within the self):**
- Happiness = harmony in the self; unhappiness = contradiction.
- Inner activities (realization, understanding) should guide outer ones (thought, desire, selection).
- Sanskar (formed by understanding) guides behaviour; self-exploration corrects preconditioning.

**Conducive social order: five dimensions (draw the flow)**

| Dimension | Meaning | Programme |
|---|---|---|
| Education–sanskar (shiksha–sanskar) | Right understanding and its practice | Education that includes values, self-exploration, skills |
| Health–sanyam (swasthya–sanyam) | Self-regulation for health of the body | Nutrition, hygiene, natural medicine, sanyam |
| Production–work (utpadan–karya) | Producing physical facilities with nature | Cyclic, eco-friendly, adequate production |
| Justice–preservation (nyaya–suraksha) | Justice in relationships, preservation of nature | Conflict resolution by understanding, protection of resources |
| Exchange–storage (vinimay–kosh) | Fair exchange and storage for future use | Exchange for mutual fulfilment, not profit-maximization; storage for need, not accumulation |

**Steps from family to world family:** family (≈ 10 members) → family cluster → village/locality → … → nation → world family; at each level the five dimensions work, with representatives chosen for their understanding and commitment.

**Self-study (svadhyay):**
1. Take the proposal (e.g. "trust is the foundation of relationships").
2. Verify it on your natural acceptance.
3. Check it in living (experiential validation): does it lead to mutual happiness?
4. If yes, it becomes your understanding; live by it.
5. Observe your own thoughts, desires and selections; notice where they come from (sensation, preconditioning, or understanding).

### Examples and comparisons

**Example 1: Justice at home.** Two siblings fight over a phone. Instead of a parent punishing one (order by fear), the parent helps them see they are related and both want to be happy; they work out a schedule. Mutual happiness = justice.

**Example 2: Exchange.** A farmer and a teacher exchange produce and teaching (mutual fulfilment). When the aim becomes maximum profit, prices rise, trust falls and hoarding starts. Exchange for fulfilment keeps trust.

**Example 3: Cyclic production in an engineering setting.** A factory that reuses treated water, uses solar power and recycles metal scrap follows the natural principle; it reduces cost and pollution together.

### Key points to remember

- Three kinds of principles: natural (cyclic, mutual fulfilment), social (relationship, justice), psychological (harmony in the self).
- Five dimensions: education-sanskar, health-sanyam, production-work, justice-preservation, exchange-storage.
- Family order to world family order.
- Self-study: proposal → natural acceptance → experiential validation → living.

### Important questions

1. Explain the natural, social and psychological principles for achieving the human goal.
2. Explain the conducive social order and its five dimensions.
3. Explain justice and the steps from family order to world family order.
4. What is self-study (svadhyay)? Explain its process.
5. How does right understanding lead to a sustainable human order? Give examples.

## Unit 5: Realized Human Conduct and Present Problems

### 2-mark definitions

- **Human conduct (manaviya acharan):** behaviour in accordance with values, character and ethics, which is definite and universal for all humans with right understanding.
- **Values (mulya):** the participation of a unit in the larger order; for a human, the feelings and qualities that ensure harmony.
- **Character (charitra):** in the course, (1) wealth earned by one's own right means (svadhan), (2) fidelity in marital relationship (svanari/svapurush), (3) kindness in work and behaviour (dayapurna karya-vyavahar).
- **Ethics (naitikta):** right utilization and protection of body, mind and wealth (tan, man, dhan).
- **Continuity of happiness:** happiness that does not depend on sensations or things, because it comes from the harmony in the self based on realization.
- **Sukh, shanti, santosh, anand:** happiness (in the self, resolution), peace (harmony in thought), satisfaction (harmony in desire), bliss (in realization) — the values of the self.
- **Jeevan mulya (values of the self):** happiness, peace, satisfaction, bliss.
- **Manav mulya (human values):** perseverance, bravery, generosity, kindness, beneficence, compassion.
- **Sthapit mulya (established values):** the nine feelings in relationships (trust, respect, affection, care, guidance, reverence, glory, gratitude, love).
- **Shisht mulya (expressed values):** the ways the feelings are shown: courtesy, simplicity, gentleness, obedience, generosity, etc.

### Core concepts

**Conduct of a realized human: values + character + ethics**
- **Values** in four groups: jeevan mulya (in the self), manav mulya (human qualities), sthapit mulya (established feelings), shisht mulya (expressed values); and **vastu mulya** (value of things: utility and artistic value).
- **Character:** own-earned wealth, marital fidelity, kindness in work and behaviour.
- **Ethics:** right utilization and protection of body (health), mind (understanding, relationships) and wealth (resources, environment).
- Such conduct is definite (does not change by place or time) and universal (same for all humans).

**Continuity of happiness: why sensation-based happiness does not last**

| Source | Nature | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Sensation (taste, sight, touch) | Temporary, needs repetition, diminishing returns | Boredom, addiction, more desire |
| Feelings from others (praise, respect) | Depends on others | Insecurity |
| Pre-conditioning (beliefs, ads) | Not verified | Confusion, conflict |
| Right understanding (realization) | Within the self, verified | Continuous happiness |

**Addressing present-day problems with Madhyasth Darshan**

| Problem | Root cause (in the course's view) | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Stress, depression, addictions among youth | Happiness sought through sensation and comparison | Self-exploration, sanyam, relationships |
| Breakdown of families | Relationships handled through things and money | Recognizing feelings, mutual evaluation, trust |
| Corruption, crime, violence | Animal consciousness; accumulation; fear-based order | Education-sanskar, justice, character |
| Communalism, terrorism, wars | Divisions by belief, nation, wealth | Undivided society, world family order |
| Environmental crisis, climate change | Exploitation, non-cyclic production, unlimited wants | Cyclic production, limited needs, ethics |
| Unethical technology and profession | Skills without values | Professional ethics: competence + values |
| Inequality and poverty | Profit-driven exchange, accumulation | Fair exchange-storage, prosperity for all families |

**Role of an engineer/professional:** competence (skills) + values (right understanding) → ethical human conduct at work: people-friendly and eco-friendly technologies, fair business, safety, honesty, and production systems that are cyclic and decentralized.

### Examples and comparisons

**Example 1: Ethics at work.** An engineer is asked to sign off a bridge design with reduced steel to cut cost. Right utilization and protection of others' bodies and wealth (ethics) means refusing and proposing a safe, economical alternative. Character and values are visible in such choices.

**Example 2: Continuity of happiness.** Exam success brings joy for a day; trust with parents and friends keeps giving happiness every day. Realization-based happiness is like a perennial spring; sensation-based joy is like a single bucket of water.

### Key points to remember

- Human conduct = values + character + ethics; definite and universal.
- Values: jeevan, manav, sthapit (nine feelings), shisht, vastu.
- Character: svadhan, marital fidelity, kindness. Ethics: right use and protection of tan, man, dhan.
- Continuous happiness comes from realization, not sensation.
- Present problems trace back to lack of right understanding; answers through education-sanskar and universal human order.

### Important questions

1. Explain the conduct of a realized human: values, character and ethics.
2. Classify values according to Madhyasth Darshan.
3. How can happiness be continuous? Compare sensation-based and understanding-based happiness.
4. How can Madhyasth Darshan help address present-day problems of individuals, society and nature?
5. Explain the role of right understanding in professional ethics.
