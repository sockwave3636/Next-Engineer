# BOE076 Vision for Humane Society: One-Day Exam Revision Notes

Original revision notes written for the AKTU syllabus, which builds on the Universal Human Values course (R. R. Gaur, R. Asthana, G. P. Bagaria) and the Madhyasth Darshan of A. Nagraj. Every unit has the same parts:
1. **2-mark definitions**: short answers you can write word for word in Section A.
2. **Core concepts**: the framework, explained step by step, with tables and flow diagrams.
3. **Examples and comparisons**: everyday situations that show the ideas (useful for "explain with an example" questions).
4. **Key points to remember**.
5. **Important questions**: the long questions to prepare, with what your answer must contain.

## How to use these notes the day before the paper

- **Morning (3 hours):** Unit 1 (basic human aspiration and the program to fulfil it, family and relationships, role of work, comprehensive human goal, undivided society, universal human order, appraisal of the present and of past efforts) and Unit 2 (the nine feelings in relationships, established and expressed values, mutual evaluation, justice, culture, civilization and human conduct).
- **Afternoon (3 hours):** Unit 3 (undivided society from family to world family, continuity of culture and civilization, universal human order and its conceptual framework) and Unit 4 (the five dimensions of a human order: education-sanskar, health-sanyam, production-work, exchange-storage, justice-preservation).
- **Evening (2 hours):** Unit 5 (scope and steps towards universal human order, human tradition, transition from the present state, role of students, present efforts). Then the "Last-hour checklist".
- **In the exam:** this is a reflective course. Define each term in the course's words, draw the flow diagrams (comprehensive human goal, family order to world family order, five dimensions), and add one example from your own life or society. Avoid preaching; explain and reason.

**AKTU paper pattern (theory, 70 marks, 3 hours):** Section A has 7 short questions of 2 marks, all compulsory (14). Section B has 5 questions of 7 marks, attempt any 3 (21). Section C has 5 questions (one per unit), each with two parts, attempt one part of each, 7 marks each (35). Every unit gets its own Section C question, so do not skip any unit.

## Unit 1: Human Aspiration and the Need for an Undivided Society

### 2-mark definitions

- **Basic human aspiration:** continuous happiness and prosperity, for oneself and for others.
- **Happiness:** a state of being in harmony; being in a state of liking (in harmony) is happiness, and being in contradiction (conflict) is unhappiness.
- **Prosperity:** the feeling of having or making more than the required physical facilities. It needs right identification of needs and production of more than needed.
- **Program for fulfilment:** right understanding (in the self) + right relationship (with other humans) + physical facilities (from nature), in that order of priority.
- **Right understanding:** understanding of harmony at all four levels: in the self, in the family, in society and in nature/existence.
- **Relationship:** the connection between one human being and another, with feelings (values) that are naturally expected and that lead to mutual happiness.
- **Comprehensive human goal:** right understanding in every individual, prosperity in every family, fearlessness (trust) in society, and co-existence in nature/existence.
- **Undivided society (akhand samaj):** a society in which every human being is related to every other on the basis of right understanding and trust, without divisions of caste, religion, nation, gender or wealth.
- **Universal human order (sarvabhaum vyavastha):** an order from family to world family in which all five dimensions of human living (education, health, production, exchange, justice) work together to fulfil the comprehensive human goal.

### Core concepts

**Basic human aspiration and the program:** every human being wants to be happy and prosperous continuously. Today most effort goes into physical facilities alone, which gives only short-lived pleasure (sensory happiness) and leads to the feeling of deprivation even among the rich. The program in the right order is:

| Priority | Requirement | Where it comes from | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Right understanding | Self-exploration and education | Happiness in the self |
| 2 | Right relationship (feelings) | Living with right understanding in family and society | Mutual happiness |
| 3 | Physical facilities | Working with nature | Prosperity (when needs are rightly identified) |

**Need for family and relationship:** the family is the first place where a human learns relationships (trust, respect, affection, care). It provides physical care, emotional security and the first education. The feelings in relationships are naturally acceptable to everyone; when they are fulfilled there is mutual happiness, and when they are not, there is conflict.

**Human relationship and the role of work:** in a family each member works (physical work and work to sustain relationships). Physical work with nature produces the physical facilities for the family's needs. When needs are identified correctly (and they are limited) and production is more than needs, the family feels prosperous and can share with others. Work done with right understanding is not a burden but participation in harmony.

**Comprehensive human goal**

```text
Right understanding     →  in every individual
Prosperity              →  in every family
Fearlessness (trust)    →  in society
Co-existence            →  in nature / existence
```

**Need for an undivided society and universal human order:** when people see each other as different and hostile groups (by caste, religion, region, nation, wealth), there is fear, mistrust and conflict at every level: domestic violence, communal tension, wars, exploitation of nature. Only an undivided society based on mutual trust, and an order in which education, health, production, exchange and justice all support each other, can fulfil the human goal for all.

**Appraisal of the current state:** individuals: stress, depression, confusion about goals; families: breakdown, lack of time, conflict; society: inequality, corruption, crime, communalism, terrorism; nations: wars, arms race; nature: pollution, climate change, resource depletion, extinction. Cause: lack of right understanding, so we try to get happiness from things and try to fulfil relationships with things (money, gifts).

**Appraisal of past efforts in human history**

| Effort | Main idea | Limitation (in the course's view) |
|---|---|---|
| Religion-based order | Faith, rituals, moral rules | Based on belief, not understanding; divided humanity into groups |
| Power-based (monarchy, empire) | Order by force | Fear, not trust |
| Wealth-based (capitalism) | Prosperity through accumulation and market | Inequality, exploitation, consumerism, environmental damage |
| State control (communism, socialism) | Equality through state ownership | Suppression of individual, fear, did not address human understanding |
| Democracy and rights | Rule of law, rights, elections | Rights without responsibility; competition and division remain |
| Social reformers (Gandhi, Lohia, JP, Deendayal Upadhyaya) | Swaraj, decentralization, integral humanism, total revolution | Valuable directions but not fully realized as a universal order |

The lesson: an order based on fear, belief or wealth cannot be stable. It needs right understanding in every individual.

### Examples and comparisons

**Example 1: Pleasure vs happiness.** A student buys a new phone and feels good for a few days (sensory pleasure from a physical facility). The feeling fades and a new desire comes. When the same student resolves a long misunderstanding with a friend and trust is restored, the happiness continues as long as the relationship is fulfilled. Physical facilities are necessary but not sufficient.

**Example 2: Prosperity vs wealth.** A family earning ₹30,000 a month who has identified its needs and saves and shares feels prosperous. A family earning ₹3 lakh a month that keeps wanting more feels deprived. Prosperity is a feeling based on right identification of needs, not the amount of wealth.

### Key points to remember

- Basic aspiration: continuous happiness and prosperity.
- Program: right understanding → relationship → physical facilities.
- Comprehensive goal: understanding (individual), prosperity (family), fearlessness (society), co-existence (nature).
- Undivided society; universal human order.
- Past orders based on fear, belief, wealth or force have not given stability.

### Important questions

1. What is the basic human aspiration? Explain the program for its fulfilment.
2. Explain the need for family and relationships and the role of work.
3. Explain the comprehensive human goal with a diagram.
4. Why is an undivided society and universal human order needed? Appraise the current state of society.
5. Appraise past efforts in human history to achieve a human order.

## Unit 2: Human-Human Relationship and Justice

### 2-mark definitions

- **Feeling (bhaav):** the value in a relationship that one person has for another, such as trust or respect.
- **Established values:** the feelings that are naturally inherent in a relationship and are definite: the nine feelings (trust, respect, affection, care, guidance, reverence, glory, gratitude, love).
- **Expressed values:** the ways in which these feelings are shown in behaviour and work: in words, actions and help.
- **Trust (vishwas):** the foundational value; to be assured that the other intends always to make me happy and prosperous.
- **Respect (samman):** right evaluation: seeing the other as similar to me, and appreciating our complementary differences.
- **Affection (sneh):** the feeling of being related to the other, that the other is a relative.
- **Care (mamta):** the feeling to nurture and protect the body of the relative.
- **Guidance (vatsalya):** the feeling to ensure right understanding and feelings in the self of the relative.
- **Reverence (shraddha):** the feeling of acceptance of excellence in the other.
- **Glory (gaurav):** the feeling for someone who has made efforts for excellence for others.
- **Gratitude (kritagyata):** the feeling for those who have helped me in my progress.
- **Love (prem):** the complete value; the feeling of being related to all.
- **Mutual evaluation:** both persons in a relationship evaluating whether the feelings are being fulfilled, leading to mutual happiness.
- **Justice (nyaya):** the recognition of relationship, its fulfilment, and right evaluation of that fulfilment, leading to mutual happiness.

### Core concepts

**Recognition of human-human relationship:** relationships already exist; we do not create them, we only need to recognize them. Recognition depends on understanding the self (I) as the one who has feelings and the other as also a self with the same basic aspiration.

**The nine feelings and their meaning**

| Feeling | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Trust | Foundation: the other wants my happiness, as I want theirs |
| Respect | Right evaluation; seeing similarity, accepting complementarity |
| Affection | Feeling related; the other is mine |
| Care | Nurturing and protecting the other's body |
| Guidance | Ensuring right understanding in the other |
| Reverence | Accepting the other's excellence |
| Glory | For those who strive for others' excellence |
| Gratitude | For those who helped me |
| Love | Being related to all: the complete value |

**Established vs expressed values:** established values (feelings) are the same for everyone; expressed values are the ways they come out in behaviour (for example, care is expressed by cooking for a sick family member). When expression matches feeling, relationships are fulfilled; when we express without the feeling (formality), relationships feel hollow.

**Interrelatedness of feelings and their fulfilment:** trust is the base; without trust, respect becomes flattery, care becomes control. The feelings support each other and together lead to love. Fulfilment happens when (1) both recognize the relationship, (2) both have the feelings, (3) both express them, and (4) both evaluate and feel mutual happiness.

**Types of relationships and their purpose:** parent-child, siblings, spouses, teacher-student, friends, colleagues, citizens. Every relationship has the same purpose: mutual happiness through mutual fulfilment of feelings, and mutual development (each helping the other to grow in understanding).

**Justice in relationships:** justice = recognition of the relationship + fulfilment of feelings + right evaluation + mutual happiness. Justice starts in the family and extends to society and the world. Without justice in relationships, people seek justice from courts, which can only punish and not restore trust.

**Justice leads to culture, civilization and human conduct**

```text
Justice in relationships
   → Culture (sanskriti): the way of thinking and doing based on right understanding
   → Civilization (sabhyata): systems and practices that express this culture in society
   → Human conduct (manaviya acharan): values + policy + character, shown definitely in behaviour
```

**Human conduct (definite conduct):** values (feelings in relationships), policy (right use of physical facilities: enrichment, protection and right utilization of the body, mind and wealth), character (chastity in conjugal relations, rightful earning, kindness in behaviour).

### Examples and comparisons

**Example 1: Respect as right evaluation.** In a project team, one member is brilliant at coding and another at presentation. Over-evaluating the coder ("only he matters") or under-evaluating the presenter is disrespect. Respect means recognizing both as equal selves with the same aspiration and valuing their complementary skills.

**Example 2: Trust in intention vs competence.** A younger brother promises to help but forgets. If I doubt his intention ("he doesn't care"), the relationship suffers. If I recognize his intention is good but his competence (memory, planning) needs help, I can guide him, and trust remains.

### Key points to remember

- Nine feelings; trust is foundational; love is complete value.
- Established (feelings) vs expressed (behaviour) values.
- Justice = recognition + fulfilment + evaluation → mutual happiness.
- Justice → culture → civilization → human conduct.
- Human conduct = values + policy + character.

### Important questions

1. Explain the recognition of human-human relationships and the role of feelings.
2. Explain the nine feelings in relationships. Distinguish established and expressed values.
3. Explain the interrelatedness of feelings, their fulfilment and mutual evaluation.
4. Explain the types of relationships and their purpose.
5. What is justice in relationships? How does it lead to culture, civilization and human conduct?

## Unit 3: Undivided Society and Universal Human Order

### 2-mark definitions

- **Family order (parivar vyavastha):** a family living with right understanding, mutual fulfilment and prosperity, as the smallest unit of human order.
- **World family order (vishwa parivar vyavastha):** the extension of family order through the village and nation to the whole world, with the same feelings.
- **Continuity of culture:** passing right understanding and values from generation to generation through education and conduct.
- **Continuity of civilization:** sustaining systems (education, health, production, exchange, justice) that express that culture in society.
- **Universal order:** an order that holds for everyone and everywhere, because it is based on the reality of harmony in existence.
- **Order in existence:** existence (nature and all units in space) is already in harmony; the four orders (material, plant/bio, animal, human) are interconnected and mutually fulfilling. Only humans are not yet in order because of lack of right understanding.

### Core concepts

**Undivided society as continuity and expansion of justice:**

```text
Family → Family cluster → Village / community → Region → Nation → World family
```

Justice that begins in the family (mutual fulfilment) expands to wider relationships. A person who has trust and respect for family members can extend the same feelings to neighbours, colleagues, citizens of other countries and future generations. This is the basis of an undivided society: the same feelings for all, without divisions.

**Continuity of culture and civilization:** culture is carried by education (sanskar) and lived conduct; civilization is carried by systems. If education passes only skills (and not values), culture breaks; if systems do not serve human goals (for example, production that damages nature), civilization becomes unsustainable.

**Universal order based on undivided society:** when every individual has right understanding, families are prosperous, society is fearless, and humans live in co-existence with nature. The order is "universal" because it is based on what is naturally acceptable to every human being (feelings in relationships, harmony with nature), not on any particular religion, culture or ideology.

**Conceptual framework for universal human order**

| Level | Goal | System |
|---|---|---|
| Individual | Right understanding | Education with sanskar |
| Family | Prosperity | Health, production, exchange within the family |
| Society | Fearlessness (trust) | Justice, preservation, participation |
| Nature | Co-existence | Production and preservation in harmony with nature |

The five dimensions of human order (education-sanskar, health-sanyam, production-work, exchange-storage, justice-preservation) operate at every level from family to world family.

**Universal human order as continuity of order in living:** the four orders in nature are mutually fulfilling (material order supports plants; plants feed animals and humans; animals enrich soil). Human order means humans also become mutually fulfilling with the other three orders and with each other, so the whole existence remains in order continuously.

### Examples and comparisons

**Example 1: Expansion of justice.** A family that resolves disputes through dialogue, shares resources fairly and cares for elders becomes a model in its neighbourhood; neighbourhood committees adopt similar practices for water sharing; a village panchayat applies them to common resources; this shows justice expanding from family to society.

**Example 2: Divided vs undivided view.** In a divided view, a person in another country is "foreign", "competitor" or "enemy". In an undivided view, that person is also a human being with the same aspiration for happiness and prosperity, so cooperation (climate agreements, disaster aid) becomes natural.

### Key points to remember

- Undivided society = continuity and expansion of justice from family to world family.
- Culture (understanding and values) and civilization (systems) must continue together.
- Universal order is based on what is naturally acceptable to all.
- Framework: individual (understanding), family (prosperity), society (fearlessness), nature (co-existence).
- Four orders in existence; humans must become mutually fulfilling.

### Important questions

1. Explain undivided society as the continuity and expansion of justice from family to world family.
2. Explain the continuity of culture and civilization.
3. Explain universal human order based on an undivided society.
4. Describe the conceptual framework for universal human order.
5. Explain universal human order as the continuity of order in living.

## Unit 4: Programs for an Undivided Society

### 2-mark definitions

- **Education-sanskar (shiksha-sanskar):** education that develops right understanding (shiksha) and the commitment and practice of living with it (sanskar).
- **Health-sanyam (swasthya-sanyam):** health as harmony between the self and the body; sanyam is the self's responsibility to nurture, protect and rightly use the body.
- **Production-work (utpadan-karya):** producing physical facilities through work with nature, cyclically and in harmony, to fulfil needs.
- **Exchange-storage (vinimay-kosh):** exchanging surplus for mutual fulfilment (not for profit-making alone) and storing surplus for future needs.
- **Justice-preservation (nyaya-suraksha):** ensuring justice in relationships between humans, and protecting and enriching nature (preservation).
- **Five dimensions of human order:** the five systems above, which together sustain human order at all levels.

### Core concepts

**The five dimensions and what they ensure**

| Dimension | Ensures | Today's distortion | Humane direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Education-sanskar | Right understanding and right feelings in every individual | Skill-only, marks-driven education | Value education with skills; self-exploration |
| Health-sanyam | Healthy body under the self's responsibility | Treatment-centred, lifestyle diseases | Prevention, balanced diet, exercise, self-discipline |
| Production-work | Enough physical facilities, produced cyclically with nature | Mass production, waste, pollution | Need-based, renewable, local, natural farming |
| Exchange-storage | Mutual fulfilment through sharing surplus | Profit maximization, hoarding, exploitation | Fair exchange, cooperatives, storage for common needs |
| Justice-preservation | Justice in relationships and protection of nature | Punitive justice, exploitation of resources | Restorative justice, conservation, enrichment of nature |

**Education-sanskar:** content: understanding of harmony at all four levels (self, family, society, nature) and skills for producing physical facilities. Process: self-exploration, dialogue, verification on one's own right. Outcome: an individual who is self-confident, responsible and committed to human goals.

**Health-sanyam:** the self is the user and the body is the instrument. Sanyam means the self taking responsibility for nurturing (food, rest), protecting (hygiene, safety) and rightly utilizing (purposeful work) the body. Many diseases arise from lack of sanyam (stress, overeating, addiction).

**Production-work:** identify needs correctly (food, clothing, shelter, instruments), produce them through work with nature using cyclic and mutually enriching processes (organic farming, renewable energy), and avoid producing what harms people or nature. Work is participation in the larger order.

**Exchange-storage:** exchange is for mutual fulfilment, not for exploiting the other. Storage is to meet future and common needs (grain banks, savings), not for accumulation and hoarding. Money is a means of exchange and should not become a goal.

**Justice-preservation:** justice in human relationships (Unit 2) at all levels, including dispute resolution through dialogue (restorative justice); preservation means enrichment, protection and right use of nature (forests, water, soil, animals).

### Examples and comparisons

**Example 1: Production-work in a village.** A village switches from chemical-intensive monoculture to mixed natural farming, uses cow dung for biogas and manure, harvests rainwater, and sells surplus through a cooperative at fair prices. Needs are met, soil improves, debt falls: production in harmony with nature, and exchange for mutual fulfilment.

**Example 2: Health-sanyam for a student.** Late nights, junk food and no exercise lead to poor health. Taking responsibility (regular sleep, home food, daily walk, limiting screen time) is sanyam: the self deciding rightly for the body.

### Key points to remember

- Five dimensions: education-sanskar, health-sanyam, production-work, exchange-storage, justice-preservation.
- Each has a present distortion and a humane direction.
- Exchange for mutual fulfilment, storage for future needs, not hoarding.
- Justice in human relations plus preservation of nature.

### Important questions

1. Explain the five dimensions of human order needed for an undivided society.
2. Explain education-sanskar and health-sanyam.
3. Explain production-work and exchange-storage.
4. Explain justice-preservation.

## Unit 5: Steps Towards Universal Human Order

### 2-mark definitions

- **Scope of universal human order:** the whole of humanity, from the individual and family to the world family, and all four orders of nature.
- **Human tradition (manviya parampara):** the continuity, from generation to generation, of right understanding, feelings and systems that sustain human order.
- **Transition:** the gradual movement from the present state (based on assumptions, fear and competition) to a humane state (based on understanding, trust and cooperation).
- **Student participation:** students exploring and living the values themselves, and contributing to the transition through their family, profession and society.
- **Holistic technology / people-friendly and eco-friendly technology:** technology that serves human needs, is affordable and safe, and is in harmony with nature.

### Core concepts

**Scope and steps of universal human order:**

1. Self-exploration by individuals to develop right understanding.
2. Living in relationship in the family with trust and respect.
3. Family-based clusters and villages organizing education, health, production, exchange and justice.
4. Linking villages and cities into regional and national systems based on the same principles.
5. A world family order where nations cooperate rather than compete.

**Human tradition from family order to world family order:** a human tradition carries four things: (1) right understanding (through education), (2) right feelings (through relationships), (3) right conduct (definite human conduct), (4) right systems (the five dimensions). Each generation must receive, live and pass these on.

**Steps for transition from the current state**

| Level | Step |
|---|---|
| Individual | Self-exploration, verifying values, living with responsibility |
| Family | Dialogue, quality time, shared responsibilities, prosperity through right needs |
| Education | Include value education (UHV) in curricula; teachers as guides |
| Profession | Ethical practice, people-friendly and eco-friendly technologies |
| Economy | Local production, cooperatives, fair trade, circular economy |
| Governance | Decentralization (gram swaraj), participatory decisions, restorative justice |
| Global | Cooperation on climate, peace, disarmament, sharing knowledge |

**Student participation:** a student can start with themselves (self-exploration, healthy habits), the family (better relationships), the campus (respectful culture, no ragging, sustainable practices), the profession (choosing ethical work, designing appropriate technology) and the community (volunteering, awareness, rural projects).

**Present efforts in this direction**

- AICTE's Universal Human Values (UHV) course in all technical institutions, and value education programmes in universities.
- Natural farming movements and organic farming.
- Self-help groups, cooperatives (Amul), fair trade.
- Decentralized renewable energy and appropriate technology (Barefoot College).
- Restorative justice and community mediation (Lok Adalats).
- Global efforts: Sustainable Development Goals, climate agreements, peace movements.
- Thinkers: Gandhi (Hind Swaraj, trusteeship), J. C. Kumarappa (economy of permanence), E. F. Schumacher (small is beautiful), Deendayal Upadhyaya (integral humanism), Jayaprakash Narayan (total revolution), A. Nagraj (Madhyasth Darshan).

### Examples and comparisons

**Example 1: Transition in a profession.** An engineer designing a water purifier chooses a design that works without electricity, uses local materials, has no wasted reject water and is affordable for villages. This is a step from profit-only technology to people-friendly and eco-friendly technology.

**Example 2: Campus transition.** Students start a "no waste" mess campaign: they measure food waste, adjust portions, compost leftovers for the campus garden, and the savings fund scholarships. It combines production, exchange, preservation and relationship.

### Key points to remember

- Steps: individual → family → village/city → nation → world family.
- Human tradition carries understanding, feelings, conduct and systems.
- Transition at all levels: individual, family, education, profession, economy, governance, global.
- Student participation from self to community.
- Present efforts: UHV, natural farming, cooperatives, SDGs, thinkers.

### Important questions

1. Explain the scope and steps of universal human order.
2. Explain human tradition from family order to world family order.
3. Explain the steps of transition from the current state to universal human order.
4. How can students participate in this transition? Describe present efforts in this direction.
